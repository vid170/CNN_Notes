Transfer Learning — Concise Notes

Why Transfer Learning?

CNNs are hard to train from scratch for every new task because:

  • Optimization is hard: deep nets need careful init + heavy hyperparameter tuning.
  • Overfitting risk: params (millions) >> typical data samples.
    • AlexNet: 60M | VGG: 138M | Inception V1: 5M | Inception V3: 23M | ResNet-50: 25M
  • Training is slow/expensive: AlexNet ~1 week (2 GPUs), VGG ~2–3 weeks (4 GPUs), GoogLeNet ~1 week.
  • Designing a new architecture from scratch for every new domain doesn't scale.

Key Observation: Hierarchical Feature Learning in CNNs

  • Early layers → generic, low-level features (edges, color blobs) — task/domain independent.
  • Middle layers → mid-level features (textures, combinations of edges/colors).
  • Later layers → high-level, task-specific features (object parts/impressions).

➡️ Since early layers are generic, they can often be reused across tasks — this is the foundation of transfer learning.

Fine-Tuning Strategies (by dataset size & domain similarity)

Data SizeDomain SimilarityStrategyReasoning
Small Similar Freeze entire pretrained network; train only the final classification layer Both generic & specialized features likely transfer; too little data to safely retrain feature layers
Small Dissimilar Use pretrained net as fixed feature extractor (early/generic layers only) → feed features into a separate classifier (e.g., SVM or small NN) Generic features (edges/blobs) still transfer, but specialized features don't; not enough data to learn new filters
Large Similar Fine-tune the whole network, but use a low learning rate Enough data to safely update all weights; low LR prevents destroying useful pretrained weights
Large Dissimilar Can train from scratch (random/He/Glorot init) OR use transitive transfer learning Enough data to learn everything from scratch; pretrained features may not even be well-suited (different low-level statistics) → risk of negative transfer; no strong benefit to constraining init

My Doubt — Clarified

Q: For large+dissimilar data, won't early-layer features (edges/shapes) still be useful since they're common to all images?

  • Partly true, but "generic" filters are generic for natural images — very different domains (X-rays, spectrograms, satellite images) may have different low-level statistics too.
  • With large data, you don't need the shortcut pretrained weights give you (data efficiency) — you have enough samples to learn good filters yourself.
  • Risk of negative transfer: initializing from a very different source task can bias optimization toward a suboptimal solution.
  • Training from scratch also allows freedom to adjust architecture, not just weights.

Q: Do we avoid training from scratch on small datasets because of overfitting?

  • Yes — exactly. Small data + full training = too many parameters (millions) to estimate from too few samples → severe overfitting, and even early filters may fail to learn cleanly.
  • Guiding principle: minimize the number of parameters the small dataset has to learn.
